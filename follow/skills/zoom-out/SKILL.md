# zoom-out - the shape of the work, from above

You have been down in the detail for a while.
This comes up for air - the shape of everything worked on so far, short enough to take in at once, aimed at what to do next.

The reader was here the whole time.
Nothing needs introducing - name things in passing and move on.
If it reads like it's explaining, it's at the wrong altitude.

Four things the instinct gets wrong:

- **Shape, not sequence.** Not what happened in what order - what holds now, what is still open, and how the pieces sit against each other.
- **Name the drift.** When the work wandered off what it set out to be, say so. The instinct is to present it as one coherent plan it never was.
- **One screen.** A picture you can't take in at a glance isn't a picture. Cut the depth, not the coverage.
- **End on the move.** This exists to unstick, so it closes on the next thing to do and what that waits on.
